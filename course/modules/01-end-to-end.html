One change, end to end

What this platform is for, told through the story of one person changing their email address.

What are we building?

Somewhere there is an app with users. Its database, MongoDB, is busy serving that app, so it is not the place to run heavy reports.

This project copies every change from that database into a separate lakehouse, cleans it up in stages, and produces business numbers like "signups per day". The stages are called bronze, silver and gold. That three-stage pattern is the medallion architecture.

🥉

Bronze

Every change, exactly as it happened. Never edited, never deleted. Only ever appended to.

🥈

Silver

One row per user, showing their current state. Duplicates removed, deletes flagged.

🥇

Gold

Summaries a business person asks for: users by country, daily signups, users per status.

The bank-statement way of thinking

Your bank keeps a statement (every single transaction, forever), shows you a balance (where you stand right now) and sends a yearly summary (spending by category). That is bronze, silver and gold.

🥉

💡
Why keep the raw statement at all?

If the balance is ever wrong, say from a bug in the cleaning code, you can rebuild it from the statement. Keeping an untouched, append-only history is what makes every later layer re-buildable. When you brief an AI, say "bronze is append-only, never mutate it".

That whole technique, copying every change out of a database as it happens, is called CDC (Change Data Capture).

What a Change Event actually looks like

This is a design sketch, not code from the repo yet (nothing is built). It shows the shape of one event Debezium produces for Ana.

DESIGN SKETCH 
{
  "op": "u",
  "after": "{\"_id\":\"66a1…\",\"email\":\"ana@new.com\",\"status\":\"active\"}",
  "source": { "ts_ms": 1762370000123, "ord": 4 },
  "offset": 8812
}
PLAIN ENGLISH

One event, wrapped in curly braces (that's JSON).

What happened: u = update. Others are c create, d delete, r read during the first snapshot.

The whole user document after the change, packed as text. New fields can appear here without breaking anything.

When it happened (milliseconds) and its order within that moment. Module 3 is all about these two numbers.

Its position in the Kafka queue. Used to remember "how far have I read?"

End of the event.
